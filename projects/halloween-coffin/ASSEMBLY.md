# Rise and Shine: crank coffin assembly

These instructions cover the crank coffin itself: the box, the lid, the rising
figure and the crank mechanism. They leave out the optional extras (the swinging
arms, the leg false floor and the separate little skeleton). The coffin works
the same without them.

## Parts

All files are in `stl/`, already in print orientation. No supports.

| File | Print | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `coffin_box.stl` | 1 | The coffin body |
| `coffin_lid.stl` | 1 | The lid with the crescent moon |
| `coffin_torso.stl` | 1 | The rising figure, with two rocker cheeks on its back |
| `coffin_conrod.stl` | 1 | The connecting rod |
| `coffin_crank.stl` | 1 | The crank web, with a small stub sticking up |
| `coffin_handle.stl` | 1 | The crank handle |
| `coffin_pins.stl` | 1 | Six pins: three for this build, the 5 mm crank shaft, and two short shoulder pins you won't need |

The pins you need, longest first:

| Pin | Length | Goes through |
|---|---|---|
| Hip pin | 54 mm | Both box walls and the figure's hip |
| Crank shaft | 24.5 mm, 5 mm thick with a flat side | Handle, box wall and crank web |
| Lid pin | 24 mm | The three hinge knuckles |
| Fork pin | 10 mm | The two rocker cheeks and the rod's small end |

## Before you start

Check that each pin slides through the holes it should turn in, and grips in
the ones it shouldn't. Printed holes often come out a little small.

- **Should turn freely:** the hip hole in the figure, the small-end hole in the
  rod, the lid's middle knuckle, the rod's big end on the crank stub, and the
  crank shaft in the box wall. If one binds, run a 3.5 mm drill bit through it by
  hand. Use a 5.5 mm bit for the crank-shaft hole in the box wall.
- **Should grip:** the hip holes in the box walls, the holes in the rocker
  cheeks, and the box's two outer hinge knuckles. A firm push is right here. If a
  pin won't go in at all, ream it lightly with a 3.2 mm bit.
- **Press fits:** the crank shaft's flat side presses into both the crank web
  and the handle. It should take a firm push. If it's loose, a drop of glue
  fixes it.

## Assembly

The crank goes on the left-hand side when you stand at the foot end (the end
with the hinge) and look toward the head. Call that side the crank side; the
square pad on the outside of the box marks it.

1. **Figure in.** Lay the figure in the box face up,
   head toward the end without the hinge, with the rocker cheeks underneath,
   pointing toward the feet and offset toward the crank side. Line its hip hole
   up with the holes in the box walls.
2. **Hip pin.** Push the hip pin in from the side away from the crank until its
   head sits flush in the countersink. The figure should now tip up and down
   freely on the pin.
3. **Rod onto the crank.** Slip the rod's big end (the larger hole) over the
   stub on the crank web. Lower the web and rod into the foot end of the box,
   with the web just inside the bearing block on the crank side and the stub
   pointing into the box.
4. **Rod to the figure.** Tip the figure up a little, swing the rod's small end
   in between the two rocker cheeks, and push the fork pin through the cheeks and
   the rod. Its head goes on the outer cheek. The cheeks now hold the rod on the
   stub, so it can't slide off.
5. **Crank shaft and handle.** Slide the handle onto the crank shaft, flat side
   to flat side, until it sits against the shaft's head. Push the shaft in from
   outside through the square pad and the bearing block. Turn the web inside until
   its D-shaped hole lines up with the flat, then press the shaft home. The
   shaft's tip ends flush with the inside face of the web.
6. **Test the crank.** Before fitting the lid, turn the handle a few times. The
   figure should rise smoothly from lying flat to about 55° and back once per
   turn. If it sticks, find the tight joint (usually the fork pin or the rod on
   its stub) and ease that hole.
7. **Lid.** Set the lid on with its middle knuckle between the box's two
   knuckles at the foot end. Push the lid pin through all three from the side
   away from the crank. The lid is notched to clear the pin's head.

## Using it

Turn the handle. As the figure sits up, the top of its head pushes the lid open,
and as it lies back down the lid falls shut under its own weight. Nothing needs
lubricating, but a little dry PTFE spray on the pins makes it quieter.

## If something goes wrong

- **The crank turns but the figure doesn't move.** The crank web has slipped on
  the shaft. Glue it, or print the shaft again a touch thicker.
- **The figure stops partway.** The rod is rubbing the crank web or a cheek.
  Check it has a little side play, and ease the rod's big end if needed.
- **The lid doesn't close.** Check the lid pin turns freely in the lid's
  middle knuckle, and that nothing is resting on the figure.
